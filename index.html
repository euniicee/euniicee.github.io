What makes me, me

I'm a Software Engineering student at Trent University, and probably the most easily distracted person you'll meet, in the best way. Give me a stack trace and I'll treat it like a case file: gather the evidence, follow the clues, land on the fix. That's the same energy I bring to K-dramas, thrillers, and pretty much anything with a decent plot twist.

Outside coursework I build things I actually want to use, like a K-drama recommender for my own addiction, and I run the Software Engineering Club I founded because I wanted this program to feel a little less quiet and a lot more fun. Lately I've also been digging into AI, not just using it, but figuring out how to use it well as a software engineer: where it genuinely saves time, where it gets in the way of actually understanding your own code, and how to stay the one steering instead of just along for the ride.

I got my start troubleshooting hardware and networks as an IT intern before I ever wrote a line of production code, so I still think like someone who has to actually fix the thing, not just ship it. That mix, part investigator, part builder, part person who will absolutely make you laugh mid-standup, is what I bring to a team: I take the work seriously and myself not so much.

Case study

M나NE, up close

The problem

I was tired of scrolling through generic top-10 lists that never matched what I actually wanted to watch. I wanted something personalized, built around my own taste, not a stranger's.

The approach

I built the whole pipeline myself: took a raw dataset of 2,000+ titles into a MySQL database, wrote the backend logic in Python, and built the React/TypeScript frontend on top. Added user authentication and a rating system so the recommendations actually improve the more you use it.

What's next

Right now it's local-only. Next up is getting it in front of other people to test, tightening the recommendation logic to weigh ratings more meaningfully, and wrapping it in a proper API layer.
